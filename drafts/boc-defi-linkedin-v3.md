Settled.

The Bank of Canada just published a formal research paper on Aave V3. The largest decentralized lending protocol in the world. Their conclusion: it works.

Zero non-performing loans across all of 2024. A 0.64% net interest margin by design. 24/7 operations with near-zero overhead. 83% of revenue from three tokens. 80% of all liquidations concentrated in just 10 events.

That word, "settled," reads differently depending on where you sit.

If you work in finance, settlement means finality. The moment a transaction clears and the books balance. In traditional lending, that process takes days. Reconciliation, clearing houses, correspondent banks. On Aave, settlement is the transaction. One block. Done. The Bank of Canada just confirmed that this isn't experimental anymore. It's operationally viable.

If you work in law, settled means resolved. No more open questions. For years, the legal status of decentralized lending sat in a gray zone. Is it securities? Is it banking? Who has jurisdiction over a smart contract? When a G7 central bank publishes a 40-page study treating DeFi as a functioning financial system, not a curiosity, that shifts the baseline of every regulatory conversation going forward.

If you work in tech, settled means immutable. Every transaction on Aave is permanently recorded on the Ethereum blockchain. No amendments, no reversals, no "we'll get back to you." Shermin Voshmgir described this beautifully in Token Economy: tokens aren't just currency. They're programmable rights. Aave doesn't lend money the way a bank does. It executes economic logic through code. The loan application, the approval, the disbursement, the liquidation if you miss your margin. All of it is one continuous program. Code is the law. Not a metaphor. The actual mechanism.

I started using Aave six years ago. The recursive leverage the report describes, depositing ETH, borrowing stablecoins, converting back, depositing again, I was doing exactly that. I learned every lesson they documented. Except I learned them with my own money, in real time, with no report to guide me.

Every one of those transactions is still on the blockchain. Timestamped. Permanent. Verifiable by anyone who wants to look.

The real story here isn't one system replacing another. Traditional finance has enormous strengths. Relationships, trust, the ability to sit across from someone and understand their life before approving a loan. DeFi has different strengths. Transparency, programmability, composability that lets protocols build on each other like software.

The people who understand both worlds will build the next generation of financial infrastructure. Not because one side wins. Because the bridge between them is where the interesting work lives.

The Bank of Canada just went on the record. The question is settled.
