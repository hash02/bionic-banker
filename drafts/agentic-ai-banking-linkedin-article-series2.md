In Part 1 of this series, I shared the headline: $67 billion market, only 21% in production. That number sparked a question I kept hearing: "okay but where is the proof that banks are actually doing this?"

Fair enough. So I went looking. Not at vendor marketing. Not at analyst predictions. At what banks are actually shipping right now, in April 2026, across four countries.

What I found is that the gap between "we use AI" and "we have autonomous agents running in production" is not closing evenly. Some countries are sprinting. Some are walking carefully. And the ones walking carefully have good reasons, but the window to define the standard is shrinking.

**The US: Infrastructure to Build Infrastructure**

JPMorgan doesn't have an AI project. They have 500 AI use cases in production, building to 1,000 by year end. Their COiN platform reads 12,000 credit agreements in seconds, saving 360,000 legal hours annually. Compliance errors dropped 80%. AML false positives slashed 95%. They're deploying a GenAI assistant to 140,000 employees, targeting $1.5 billion in productivity value.

Wells Fargo's Fargo assistant crossed 200 million fully autonomous customer interactions. BNY Mellon's Eliza platform lets employees design their own AI agents, with 150 AI-powered offerings planned.

The pattern: US banks built the factory that builds AI tools. Not one tool. The factory.

**India: A Different Question**

HDFC Bank's Eva chatbot responds in 0.4 seconds. But the important move wasn't speed. It was deploying Eva to 127,000 Village Level Entrepreneurs on the Digital Seva Portal. Instant loan applications. Business loans. Financial services for rural India, through an agent that needs no branch and no call center.

India asked "how do we reach people we can't reach with physical infrastructure" before asking "how do we optimize existing operations." That question led to a fundamentally different kind of agent deployment.

**Australia: Bold Moves, Familiar Regulation**

ANZ became the first bank in APAC to deploy Salesforce Agentforce 360 at scale (February 2026). They collapsed 20 legacy systems into one unified dashboard. Their business bankers recovered the equivalent of one full working month per year.

What makes ANZ instructive for Canada: they operate under similar regulatory frameworks. Conservative banking culture. Big-four dominance. And they still moved to production scale. Their 2030 Strategy commits to increasing business banker headcount by 50% while improving productivity. Agents aren't replacing people. They're freeing people.

**Canada: Real Strength, Measured Pace**

The Evident AI Index 2025 put RBC at #3 globally for the fourth consecutive year. TD at #13. These are real positions. RBC's Aiden platform lets one analyst cover 50 companies instead of 15. Earnings analysis dropped from 45 to 120 minutes down to seconds. 8,000+ users. Target: C$700 million to $1 billion in AI value by 2027.

Scotiabank's AIDox handles 90% of 1,500 daily commercial emails autonomously. Been building since 2018. Upgraded to agentic in 2025. 70% of the team redeployed to higher-value work.

CIBC saved 1.2 million hours in Q1 2026 alone. 30,000 active users on CAI. 44% lift in savings account conversions. Won Best Gen-AI Initiative at Digital Banker, two years running.

The foundations are strong. The gap is scale: US banks have 500 use cases in production. Canadian banks have world-class pilots with smaller deployment footprints.

**What This Means For Your Career**

If you're in banking, the next 12 months will separate people who understand agentic AI from people who are learning about it after their workflow gets automated.

The opportunity: 99% of banks plan to deploy autonomous agents. Only 11% have. That 88% gap is where your career advantage lives, if you start building knowledge now.

Learn how agents differ from chatbots. Understand what OSFI expects in terms of governance. Follow what RBC, Scotiabank, and CIBC are publishing about their deployments. Study the JPMorgan and ANZ case studies.

The people who understand this shift before it's mainstream will be leading teams, not reporting to them.
