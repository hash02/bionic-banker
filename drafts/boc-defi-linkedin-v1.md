The Bank of Canada just published a formal research paper on Aave V3, the biggest DeFi lending protocol in the world.

They found:
→ Zero non-performing loans in 2024
→ 83% of revenue from just 3 tokens
→ 20% of borrowing is recursive leverage
→ Near-zero operational overhead
→ 24/7 operation, no branches, no staff

Their conclusion? Decentralized lending is technically and operationally viable.

This is a central bank in one of the most regulated financial systems in the world saying, out loud, that protocol-based lending works.

I wrote a technical breakdown of Aave two years ago. I was using it six years ago. Back then, I did exactly what the report now calls "recursive leverage." Borrow, swap into more collateral, borrow again.

I learned every lesson they documented. Except I learned them with my own money, in real time, with no report to warn me.

The best part? Every transaction I made is still on the blockchain. Timestamped. Permanent. Verifiable by anyone. Try getting that from a traditional bank.

We're watching a shift. Not DeFi replacing banks. Not banks killing DeFi. Something more interesting: two systems evolving for two different types of customers.

The institutions that understand AI, blockchain, AND traditional finance will lead the next decade.

The Bank of Canada just showed they understand.

Does everyone else?
