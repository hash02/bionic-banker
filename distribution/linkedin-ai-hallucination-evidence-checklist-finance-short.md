## Caption

An AI answer is not evidence.

This is the mistake I think finance teams will make with AI:

they will treat a confident summary like a reviewed source packet.

A hallucination does not always look ridiculous.

Sometimes it looks like:

- a stale policy
- a real source used badly
- a missing exception
- a confident AML note that drops the key detail
- a fraud summary that sounds right but cannot be traced

So the review question should not be:

“Does this sound right?”

It should be:

“What would prove this claim false?”

My minimum checklist:

1. exact claim
2. source trail
3. freshness
4. relevance
5. falsifier
6. reviewer
7. saved audit trail

The AI can help draft the narrative.

It should not silently become the evidence.

If you work in risk, compliance, audit, AML, fraud, or finance ops:

What AI-generated output would you never let through without a human evidence check?
