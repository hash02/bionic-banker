## Bionic filter

The provided viral hooks are useful, but several are too hype-heavy for Bionic Banker as written. Use the emotional engine, not the false claim.

Do not use:

- fake replacement claims
- fake P&L claims
- fake alpha claims
- pretending we gave an AI real money
- invented regulator/SEC claims
- screenshots we cannot verify

Use instead:

- control gap
- audit trail
- human review
- advisor workflow reality
- AI hallucination risk
- on-chain risk triage
- governance and accountability

## Ranked post ideas tailored to Bionic Banker

### 1. AI agents need gates, not vibes

**Source project:** Agent Framework Proof  
**Why it should work:** contrarian + practical + tied to a real packaged repo  
**Hook:**

> Everyone is asking what AI agents can do. Finance should ask what they are forbidden to do.

**Core point:** The serious agent demo is not the answer. It is the gate, the audit row, the blocked action, and the reviewer path.

**Asset:** build new carousel tomorrow.

---

### 2. AI is not replacing financial advisors. Advisors without audit trails are replacing themselves.

**Why it should work:** job fear, but not cheap fearmongering  
**Hook:**

> AI will not replace every financial advisor. But it will expose which advisors have no repeatable process.

**Core point:** AI helps advisors who have source trails, review processes, and documented reasoning. It hurts advisors whose value is only vague commentary.

---

### 3. The first AI finance product is not stock picking. It is review queues.

**Why it should work:** contrarian to alpha/FOMO content  
**Hook:**

> Most people are using AI in finance for the wrong first problem.

**Core point:** Before AI manages money, it should summarize documents, flag exceptions, compare claims to sources, and route cases to human review.

---

### 4. I do not want an AI advisor. I want an AI that shows its work.

**Why it should work:** relatable + high trust  
**Hook:**

> The problem with AI financial advice is not that it sounds bad. It sounds too confident.

**Core point:** Source-linked reasoning matters more than smooth language.

---

### 5. AI hallucinations in finance are not funny bugs. They are control failures.

**Why it should work:** cautionary tale + governance  
**Hook:**

> A hallucinated restaurant is annoying. A hallucinated financial rule is a risk event.

**Core point:** Finance needs claim separation: source claim, model inference, verified fact, missing context.

---

### 6. AI can detect suspicious transactions, but it cannot make the compliance decision.

**Source project:** AML Detection Engine  
**Why it should work:** concrete project + boundary  
**Hook:**

> I built a blockchain AML engine. The most important feature is what it refuses to decide.

**Core point:** The engine flags, explains, and queues. It does not accuse, freeze, file, or approve.

---

### 7. The advisor job is moving from answer-giver to evidence-reviewer.

**Why it should work:** career angle without panic  
**Hook:**

> The next finance skill is not prompting. It is reviewing evidence faster than the model can hallucinate.

**Core point:** AI compresses research time, but the human role shifts to source validation and judgment.

---

### 8. On-chain intelligence is not magic. It is messy data with better receipts.

**Why it should work:** crypto + realism  
**Hook:**

> Blockchains give finance better receipts. They do not automatically give better judgment.

**Core point:** On-chain data helps trace movement, but risk requires context, ownership, intent, and review.

---

### 9. The AI finance moat is boring: logs, receipts, limits, and review.

**Why it should work:** contrarian and brand-aligned  
**Hook:**

> The winning AI finance product may look boring from the outside.

**Core point:** Auditability beats autonomy in regulated environments.

---

### 10. Financial advisors should not fear AI. They should fear undocumented advice.

**Why it should work:** advisor-specific  
**Hook:**

> AI did not create the advice-risk problem. It made the paper trail impossible to ignore.

**Core point:** Advisors need repeatable workflows, client-specific assumptions, review notes, and source separation.

## Suggested 7-slide carousel

1. AI agents need gates, not vibes.
2. The demo is not “it answered.” The demo is “what was it allowed to do?”
3. A finance agent needs four records: request, rule, output, review.
4. The most important action is often the blocked one.
5. Local proof: retrieval, RBAC, safety eval, audit rows, trace events.
6. What it cannot do: send messages, move money, update profiles, contact third parties.
7. Takeaway: In finance, autonomy without a gate is not intelligence. It is operational risk.
